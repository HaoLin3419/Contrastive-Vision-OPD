01Overview

Abstract & §2

Vision-language models trained with on-policy distillation can produce the expected answer even when answer-relevant regions are masked or replaced with noise. This observation suggests that matching a privileged teacher's outputs does not necessarily teach the student to rely on visual evidence.

CV-OPD compares positive and negative crop-conditioned teacher distributions under the same student prefix. A Contrastive Divergence Loss (CDL) introduces a margin between the student's distances to the two teachers. A Discrepancy Aware Gate (DAG) selects token positions for contrastive supervision and excludes samples with no correct rollout.

Vision-OPD vs. CV-OPD

§2.1–3.4 · Eq. 3, 8–14

From alignment to contrastive supervision: the visual conditions change the teacher distributions, not the student-generated prefix.

Vision-OPD

Crop-conditioned distribution alignment

Original image + question

Student rollout π

Relevant crop + student prefix

Positive teacher τ+

No negative visual condition

The objective aligns π with τ+.

Positive-teacher JSDπ, τ+ → distribution alignment → student update

The relevant crop supplies focused visual supervision. Matching its teacher distribution, however, does not by itself guarantee that the student uses the same visual evidence (§2.2–2.3).

CV-OPD

Alignment + gated contrastive supervision

Original image + question

Student rollout π

Relevant crop + student prefix

Positive teacher τ+

Irrelevant crop + same prefix

Negative teacher τ−

Positive-teacher JSD + gated CDLπ, τ+, τ− → CDL + DAG → student update

Positive alignment is complemented by margin-based negative separation. DAG retains eligible samples and selects token positions for CDL. The two teacher branches share parameters.

Key difference. Vision-OPD transfers crop-conditioned knowledge through distribution alignment. CV-OPD additionally contrasts different visual conditions to encourage discrimination between relevant evidence and irrelevant cues. This is not a guarantee of grounding. 02Method

§3 · Figure 3

The student receives the full image. Two parameter-sharing teacher branches receive different crops and evaluate the same student-generated prefix.

Student and teacher inputs

§3.1 · Eq. 6–8

Student πθ

Full image + question

Generates on-policy rollouts and learns from teacher distributions along its own prefixes.

Positive Teacher τ+

Answer-relevant crop R+

Provides supervision conditioned on the region containing the evidence needed to answer.

Negative Teacher τ−

Answer-irrelevant crop R−

Provides a contrasting distribution conditioned on an unrelated region of the same image.

Shared parameters. Shared prefix. Different crops. R− matches R+ in size and satisfies IoU(R+, R−) ≤ δ. One negative crop is shared across all rollouts of each sample.

Contrastive supervision and gating

§3.2–3.4
CDL

Contrastive Divergence Loss

Pull the student toward the positive teacher and push it away from the negative teacher, until a margin is satisfied.

ℓCDL = max(0, μ + d+ − d−)Eq. 9

d± = DJS(π, τ±). The loss is zero when d− − d+ ≥ μ; the negative distribution is not pushed away indefinitely.

DAG

Discrepancy Aware Gate

Select token positions whose student–positive-teacher discrepancy exceeds the retained batch's mean.

gi,t = 𝟙[di,t > d̄B]Eq. 12

di,t = DJS(πi,t, τ+i,t). At the sample level, exclude an example if none of its rollouts is correct (Eq. 13).

Combined objective

Positive-teacher JSD + λ × gated CDL. Retained examples receive distillation; the token gate applies to the contrastive term. Student updates are followed by an EMA update of the shared teacher.

Eq. 14 · Algorithm 1

At inference: the student receives only the full image and question, without privileged crops or teacher branches.

The sample gate determines eligibility for distillation; the token gate only controls the additional CDL term. Retention does not guarantee that the teacher can correct every erroneous prefix.

From the per-token demonstration to the training objective Eq. 14 · Algorithm 1

L(θ) = ED [ b/M · Σi=1M Σt=1Tᵢ (wJSDi,t di,t + λ gi,t wCDLi,t [μ + d̃+i,t − d̃−i,t]+) ]

Equation 14 aggregates token losses over rollouts and examples. The sample gate b multiplies both terms; gi,t gates only CDL. The paper specifies normalized token weights, using 1/Ti in practice. The expectation includes examples, crop sampling and on-policy rollouts.

Algorithm 1 gives a separate normalization convention: accumulated positive-teacher distillation is divided by its weight sum N + ε, while accumulated selected-token CDL is divided by selected-token count G + ε. The slider readout is the unaggregated hinge term, not either full training loss.

Distribution support: CDL and DAG use full-vocabulary JSD. The primary distillation term uses student-aligned Top-100 + tail distributions with β = 0.5 (§4.1; Algorithm 1).

Reported settings: μ = 0.1, λ = 0.1, eight rollouts, negative-crop IoU ≤ 0.1. After the student update, the shared teacher follows ϕ ← (1 − τ)ϕ + τθ with τ = 0.05 (Appendix A.2; Algorithm 1). The two teacher branches are not independently optimized.

Why Does CV-OPD Work?

The reported ablations favor the final negative-crop construction and a batch-level discrepancy threshold. They support these design choices, but do not independently establish the proposed causal explanations.

Negative Teacher ConstructionCV-OPD: 78.22 Avg6; Random Crop: 77.20. One negative crop is shared across eight rollouts.

How to Construct an Effective Negative Teacher?

All Black
An all-black visual input provides the negative condition, replacing a sampled region with no visible scene content (§4.3.2).
Neighbor Crop
A region neighboring the target supplies the negative input. It may still contain answer-relevant information, potentially creating conflicting contrastive supervision.
Random Crop
Each rollout independently samples a negative region. The general negative-region construction draws from the same image (§3.1); the ablation paragraph does not separately restate all spatial constraints for this variant.
CV-OPD
One answer-irrelevant region from the same image is shared across all eight rollouts. Its height and width match the positive crop, with IoU(R+, R−) ≤ δ; Appendix A.2 sets δ = 0.1. Low overlap alone does not certify semantic irrelevance.

How to Select Reliable Distillation Signals?

Sample-level filtering · Eq. 13
Exclude a sample only when every rollout is incorrect. This controls both positive distillation and CDL, not just the contrastive term.
Token-level gating · Eq. 12
Within retained samples, add CDL only where student–positive-teacher full-vocabulary JSD strictly exceeds the threshold. Other valid positions still receive positive distillation.
Rollout mean vs. batch mean · Table 4
A rollout mean provides a local reference; the proposed batch mean pools valid positions across retained trajectories. The paper argues that short responses can make a rollout mean sensitive to a few positions (§4.3.3).

Evidence boundary. Table 2 removes DAG as a component; Table 4 changes the threshold reference. Neither reports an independently isolated sample-filter-only or token-gate-only comparison, so no separate effect size is assigned here.
